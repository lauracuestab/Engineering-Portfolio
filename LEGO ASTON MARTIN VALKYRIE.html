LEGO Aston Martin Valkyrie CAD Project

As part of EGR 120 – Introduction to Engineering, our four-person team reverse engineered a LEGO Aston Martin Valkyrie by measuring its components and recreating the vehicle in CATIA V5. I modeled the complete rear section of the car, including the rear spoiler assembly, integrated all team subassemblies into the final product, and produced the final rendered visualization at Daytona International Speedway.

Project Overview

This project introduced the fundamentals of reverse engineering and computer-aided design through the recreation of a LEGO Aston Martin Valkyrie using CATIA V5. The vehicle was divided into four major sections, allowing each team member to independently measure and model their assigned components while maintaining the dimensions of the original LEGO model. I was responsible for designing the complete rear section of the vehicle, including the rear bodywork and spoiler assembly, before integrating every team member's subassembly into a complete CAD assembly. The project concluded with a rendered visualization of the finished model at Daytona International Speedway, combining engineering design with technical presentation.

Engineering Process

The project followed a structured engineering design workflow beginning with reverse engineering the physical LEGO model and ending with a fully assembled digital recreation. After dividing the vehicle into individual sections, each team member developed their assigned components in CATIA V5 before the complete vehicle was assembled and rendered. The key stages of the design process are highlighted below.

Engineering Highlights

Reverse Engineering the Vehicle

The project began by examining the physical LEGO Aston Martin Valkyrie and dividing it into four primary sections. Each team member was responsible for measuring and recreating a different portion of the vehicle in CATIA V5. This approach allowed the complete model to be developed simultaneously while maintaining the proportions and geometry of the original LEGO design.

Rear Section Design

I was responsible for designing the complete rear section of the vehicle, including the rear wing. The subassembly was modeled by recreating the dimensions and connections of the original LEGO pieces while ensuring compatibility with the remaining vehicle assemblies.

Rear Spoiler Assembly

The rear spoiler was modeled as a separate subassembly to simplify the design process and improve assembly organization. Particular attention was given to the mounting features and alignment so the spoiler could be integrated seamlessly into the completed rear assembly.

Final Vehicle Assembly

Once every team member had completed their assigned components, I assembled the individual subassemblies into a single CATIA product. This required verifying part alignment, correcting interfaces between assemblies, and ensuring that the completed digital model accurately represented the original LEGO vehicle.

Assembly Verification

Orthographic and isometric views were used throughout the project to verify the accuracy of the assembled vehicle. Inspecting the model from multiple perspectives helped confirm proper alignment, overall proportions, and successful integration of the independently designed subassemblies before producing the final rendering.

Final Render

To complete the project, I created a rendered visualization of the assembled LEGO Aston Martin Valkyrie positioned at Daytona International Speedway. The render combined the finished CAD model with a recognizable local landmark, providing a professional presentation of the completed engineering design.
